## Cause 1, proven: goods on their way out count as stock

Save 88 holds each city's last report. Adam's Furniture in it: 1260 held, 1131 in the export depots, 1000 promised to FighterX2500 (deal 66), no caravans leaving. So he had 260 left over.

Deal 67 asked 1000 more and passed both checks: the in-step one, from Adam's report, and his own UI's, from his live city. Vanilla's check (`getAvailable`, 90% of the stock less what is promised) passes only from 1112 left over. To get there from 260 in the 333 game seconds after the resume, Adam's city would have had to make 852 Furniture more than it sold. Between saves 87 and 88, over 2656 game seconds, it gained 361. So production doesn't explain it.

The game's code does. A city gives goods in two steps:

1. `PSeller.remove` adds them to `promised`. `RESOURCE.owned()` subtracts `promised`, so the stock goes down at once. The world part reads this right away through the report's ledger, so two deals within one report's time already see each other.
2. Every 1/8 day, `PSeller.extract` turns `promised` into fetcher caravans and lowers `promised`. A fetcher reserves the goods in an export depot and picks them up one at a time, two per game second. `owned()` counts an export depot's goods whether reserved or not.

So from the moment the fetchers start until they've picked everything up, the goods count as stock and as promised to nobody. In that window, which lasts minutes, the same goods pass the check again. Deal 67 came in that window. The receiver's world caravan is loaded with the whole amount at once, whatever the giver's city later manages to fetch, and vanilla's fetcher simply drops what it can't find. That makes goods out of nothing.

The plan's first guess, a report taken only every 30 steps, isn't it: the ledger already reserves the goods at the step of the deal.

## Cause 2, disproved: nothing was delivered twice

- **Proposal IDs** are saved in the campaign already (the deal book's next ID, `SOSDET_DEALS`). Save 88's book reads: next ID 67, deal 66 accepted. The second "proposal 67" is the same number given again after going back to that save, in the other timeline.
- **The two deliveries** are two caravans. FighterX2500's report in save 88 shows 1000 Furniture bought by diplomacy and on its way: deal 66's caravan, in flight in that save. It arrived at step 11660. Deal 67's caravan, made at step 5767, arrived at 17350, as long a trip as deal 66's.
- **Transaction IDs** come from the step and start at 0 at every resume, but a transaction never outlives its step: the world part makes it and the same step's city part applies it, the queue is empty between steps, and no save holds one. A load starts the "applied once" count again (`appliedThrough(-1)`). The tests `TxnQueueTest.aRepeatedTransactionChangesNothing` and `aLoadedCityHoldsEveryTransactionUpToItsStep` already show a repeated one changes nothing.

So the "3000 Furniture" is three log lines across two timelines. What stayed after the resume: Adam gave 2000 and FighterX2500 got 2000, but Adam's city could spare only about 1260 plus what it made, which cause 1 explains.

## The fix (engine)

- `DealBook.spare(available, outgoing, exported)`: what a human can give of one good is vanilla's available stock less the goods on their way out that the stock still counts. Those goods are at most the city's leaving goods (the report's `OUTGOING`, vanilla's `attempting`) and at most its export depots' stock (`EXPORT_AMOUNT`). Plain Java, never below 0.
- `HumanDeals.sideRefusal` checks each good against it: in the world part from the giver's report, so every PC refuses alike; in the UI from the live city.
- A throne's sack (`assaultTerms`, SOS-92) takes its share of the same figure. It gives goods through the same code, so it had the same hole.
- No new report field, message, save field or protocol number.

While fetchers stand at a depot, the check can be stricter than needed: goods a fetcher already took from a stockpile are counted out twice. That lasts until the caravans leave the city.
