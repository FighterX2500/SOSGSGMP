## 10a: allies in the Attack against an invasion

### Found

- The raids of the 2026-11-17 playtest were invasions of a player's capital ("capital is invaded by Raiders" in the host logs, 21:55, 00:20, 01:44). The player fights them with vanilla's **Attack** in the invasion message: the sally outside the walls (SOS-145).
- In a session the Attack is the world command 7968 (`MapBattles.INVASION_SALLY`) and runs as a local battle on the defender's PC. Its battle is vanilla's `Attack` constructor: the capital's garrison and the defender's own armies within 4 tiles of the capital (`Attack.armies`, faction == player). Nobody else joins. SOS-145 chose this on purpose ("no ally prompt", batch-7.md).
- Vanilla's after-battle (`Attack.afterExit`, in the defender's city) gives the whole spoils window (`MVictory2`: loot, the invaders' gear by the share that died, and captives by race) to the defender.
- **The raider's stash** is the raider's personal treasury (`Raider.bounty`). Vanilla pays it in `MessDefeated`, when the raid's state reaches DEFEATED after the invasion listeners hear the victory. In a session that runs in the world part as the defender (raids are per human), so it already goes to the defender alone. Nothing changes there.

### The fix (SOSDET)

- **The question.** The sally command now carries the invader's faction (-1 for raiders and rebels), read on the defender's PC from its invasion. When it applies (world part, every PC, as the defender), the engine floods 4 tiles along the map from the capital, as `Attack.armies` does, and finds every other human's army there (`GameSally.near`). Each human found is asked when they are allied with the defender, at war with the invader (raiders: always), not in a local battle, not removed or frozen (`SallyLayout.asked`). If nobody is, the sally engages at once, as before.
- Otherwise the question is the open world prompt (`SallyLayout.Ask`): the asked players get the existing Join / Stay out window (`MpAllyJoin`, SOS-118), the defender's armies and theirs wait in place, and it engages at the next step once everyone answered, or after 30 s (the prompt timer). A second Attack meanwhile is skipped. While another world prompt is open, nobody is asked and the sally engages at once (one prompt at a time).
- **The battle.** At Engage (every PC), `SallyLayout` holds who brings what: each commander's armies near the capital, their divisions within the side's slots (as in a battle with allies, but the defender's whole garrison always fits, since vanilla's after-battle reads every garrison division), and each one's power. The division owners go into the roster (`BattleAllies.owners`), so each player commands their own divisions and fights with their own bonuses, and every ally's PC follows the battle as in SOS-160. The battle PC builds the allies' divisions in the world part at Engage, on its own stream, as a world battle's Engage builds its sides; every PC reseeds the world stream after it alike.
- **Patch `99-world-battles`, `Attack.java`** (extended, three hook lines): the constructor caps the defender's own army divisions at its slots (`sallyOwnDivisions`) and adds the allies' divisions after them (`sallyAllies`); the after-battle keeps the defender's share before its window (`sallySpoils`). Without allies the hooks change nothing.
- **The result.** In the world part on every PC, each ally's divisions get their survivors (a count and mean experience) directly, and only the defender's go to its city (`SALLY_RESULT`). In the defender's city vanilla computes the spoils; `sallySpoils` splits every tradable by power (`BattleRules.share`), keeps the defender's share for vanilla's window, and puts each ally's share in the city's report (new input `SALLY_SPOILS`). When the report applies, every PC ships it by caravan from the defender's capital to the ally's, as a battle's spoils travel.
- **Power** is each player's strength at Engage as the game measures it: `AD.power` of the armies they brought, plus the garrison's region power (`RD.MILITARY().power`) for the defender. Both are world values the NPC AI already reads, so every PC has the same. The remainder goes to the most power, the defender on a tie; a player whose army left the field gets nothing.

### The probe: the command and what each result means

`gradlew.bat assembleRig` in SOSDET's b13-10, then:

`python rig\rig.py city --tag SP1 --save Mogus-72-19f86f1e048-47002c-0-121c.save --no-invade --copies 1 --speeds 5 --steps 90000 --cmds @rig/cmds/soldierprobe.txt`

The `note` events in `build\rig\runs\SP1\events-c1.jsonl` are the `soldierprobe` lines. In each:

- **population, citizens:** if the spawn raises them by 200, `SOLDIER` people count as the host's population while stationed (vanilla's `SOLDIER` is of the citizen class). 10b then either takes them out of the city's counts while there, which needs hooks in the population stats, or accepts a city that looks bigger during the fight. **If they don't rise,** nothing is needed.
- **with a home, employed:** above 0 means the city's housing or work hands them out. 10b then needs another kind of person (hooks in `HTYPES` and `AIModules`). **0 for both** is what 10b needs.
- **needs (mean):** rising over the reports means they grow hungry or tired and would look for food and rest in the host's city. If so, 10b keeps their needs at zero from engine code while they are there (no hook). **Flat** is fine.
- **doing:** the occupation text. Idle or standing still unmustered, then mustering or marching once mustered, is what 10b needs. Anything else, for example wandering, looking for work, or an error in the game's log, means the battle AI alone doesn't hold them in a city.
- **division target / men / recruits:** target 200 and men 200 with recruits 0 is right. Recruits above 0 means the city sends citizens to that division; this run has no stationed registry, so it shows what the approved hook prevents.
- **the last report (after clear):** population back to the baseline and `SOLDIER` 0 means they leave cleanly.
- **A crash or an exception** in the copy's log means the kind can't live in a city at all, and 10b needs its own kind.

### Built

### Answer 3: how it is built

The owner's note proposed picking the divisions in the world part from the city's last report. Built otherwise, with the same aims and without that pick. The assault's commanders table stays as it was: army 0 is the defender's, the same on every PC, never naming a stationed division. The city picks the divisions as they come in and records them in its registry (`StationedDivs`), as in an invasion.

A stationed player's order for one of its own divisions passes the table only where the registry names that division as theirs, while their view of the city runs (`AiApplier.stationedOrder`, `Hooks.stationedOrderHere`). Only the PCs that run the defender's city have the registry, and only they apply division orders in that city. A PC that doesn't run it skips the order there, as it would anyway. The only difference between PCs is the count of skipped orders, which no hash or resync compares. A division the host made in between is never in the registry, so it is never given away. With no pick from a report, there is no window where the report and the city disagree. Who may order and select is the plain rule `StationOrders`, unit-tested.

## 10b, second version: plan for the owner's approval (2026-11-19)

The owner played the batch 13 build by hand (save 94, three players on one PC). Stationing worked. In the first raid the stationed player (Fray2, viewing the city) was never asked to Join the host's Attack and "stayed out", which is R3's problem. ### The owner's rules (2026-11-19)

1. **Always in the city.** A stationed army's soldiers are on the host's city map for as long as the army is stationed, not only during an invasion.
2. **Control.** Their player commands them while viewing the city. The view is asked for as any view is. A button in the stationing player's Armies tab lets the host command them too, for example to move them, and takes that back.
3. **Sally: ask first.** When the host presses Attack against an invasion, each stationed player is asked Join or Stay out before the battle starts, and the battle waits for the answers.
4. **No demuster.** A stationed division has no Demuster (or Muster) button in the host's city.
5. **The world army decides.** Every 120 seconds, the city's stationed divisions take their men, equipment and training from the army on the world map, which lives on its own supplies (the host's Supply tops those up). The host's city feeds nothing.
6. **The end.** The soldiers leave the city, and the army stays on the map, when the stationing ends: it is called back or sent away, or the army moves, is beaten or disbands. It also ends when the agreement ends or the two go to war, when either player is frozen, disconnected, removed or eliminated, or when the capital changes hands.

### What changes

Men the world army lost (desertion, starvation) leave the city. New recruits arrive near the throne. |

### Found while planning (2026-11-19)

- **R3's cause.** While a player views a city, their game is watch-only. `AiLog.captureOrder` drops every command from a watch-only PC except a short list: the world prompt's answer, a local battle's order and vote. The ally's Join (`WorldBattles.ALLY_ANSWER`, 7903) isn't on that list, so Fray2's Join was dropped on Fray2's own PC (`watchedDropped`). After 30 s the ask timed out as "stayed out", and the sally started without them. The fix is to let 7903 through like the other answers. "Ask first" then works as built: the battle waits for the answer or the 30 s.
- **Disconnects** need no new rule. A disconnect pauses the whole game until the host chooses (spec §11): Continue without them (removed), Freeze them (frozen), or Return to the last save (the session ends). The first two already end the stationing through `GameStations.endDue` ("away"); the third ends the session.
- **Population (A).** Vanilla counts a person in `StatsPopulation`'s counter through an `Addable`. The counter re-runs on every change of division (`StatDivision.set` removes and adds the person) and on every load (`Induvidual(FileGetter)`). The hook skips a person whose division is a stationed one in this city's registry, at both add and remove, so the count stays balanced. For a load to see the right registry, the registry's save section moves to the "before" list: it then loads ahead of the settlement's people, on the owner's PC and a viewer's alike.
- **Muster/Demuster.** The two Muster buttons go through `AiHooks.muster`. A demuster of a stationed division is refused there, so it stays mustered. The selection panel's button is greyed out while every selected division is a stationed one (patch `95-player-orders`, which already owns that file).
- **The refresh clock.** Steps are 1/60 s at 1x and longer at higher speeds. So the refresh follows the world's game time (`TIME.currentSecond()`, world state): every 120 game seconds, kept in the stations section so that loads and resyncs agree.
